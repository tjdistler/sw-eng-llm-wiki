## 2026-04-16 — Ingest: Fundamentals of Software Architecture Chapter 3 (Modularity)

**Source**: `raw/fundamentals-of-software-architecture/chapter-03-modularity.md`

Chapter 3 is the book's theoretical treatment of modularity as an **implicit architecture characteristic** — no requirement ever asks for it, yet without it no codebase survives. Richards and Ford organise the chapter around three measurement tools: cohesion (how related the parts inside a module are), coupling (dependencies between modules), and connascence (what kind of coupling and how hard to refactor). The chapter adds the academic underpinnings to the wiki's existing Newman-sourced pages: Constantine's seven-level cohesion scale and the LCOM metric, Yourdon and Constantine's afferent/efferent coupling from *Structured Design* (1979), Robert Martin's derived abstractness/instability/distance-from-main-sequence metrics, and — the chapter's most distinctive contribution — Meilir Page-Jones's connascence framework with five static types, four dynamic types, and three refactoring-guiding properties.

**Pages created** (3):
- `modularity.md` — umbrella page; Myers's 1978 lament; the logical-vs-physical definition; the physics-entropy framing; the short history of "modules" (GOTO → structured → Modula/Ada → OO); the three measurement tools; the Java 1.0 classpath anecdote; relationship to Newman's microservices framing
- `connascence.md` — the chapter's most distinctive concept. Full treatment of the five static types (CoN, CoT, CoM, CoP, CoA) ordered from weakest to strongest, the four dynamic types (CoE, CoT-timing, CoV, CoI), the three properties (strength, locality, degree), Page-Jones's three encapsulation guidelines, Jim Weirich's two rules of thumb (Rule of Degree, Rule of Locality), the unification with Structured Design's afferent/efferent coupling, and Richards and Ford's explicit flag that the 1996 framework predates microservices and will get a modern distributed-systems revision in Chapter 7

Three sources: domain concerns (stakeholder vocabulary translated into -ilities via Table 5-1), explicit requirements, and implicit domain knowledge (what every architect with domain experience knows without being told). ## 2026-04-16 — Ingest: Fundamentals of Software Architecture Chapter 6 (Measuring and Governing Architecture Characteristics)

**Source**: `raw/fundamentals-of-software-architecture/chapter-06-measuring-and-governing-architecture-characteristics.md`

Chapter 6 is the deep treatment of what Chapter 1 only named: how to measure and govern architecture characteristics so they don't silently drift. The chapter's argument runs in three steps: (1) characteristics must be defined objectively — vague composite terms like "agility" or "deployability" defeat ubiquitous language and block governance; (2) measurement sorts into three axes (operational / structural / process) with concrete metrics for each; and (3) governance is the practice of steering the project so declared characteristics hold, and its modern form is automated via fitness functions running in CI. The chapter is where fitness functions get their canonical book-length treatment — from the "any mechanism" definition through the six-axis taxonomy (atomic/holistic, triggered/continual, static/dynamic, automated/manual, temporal, domain-specific) to four concrete code examples (JDepend cycle detection, JDepend distance-from-main-sequence, ArchUnit layered-architecture rules, NetArchTest layer rules) and the Netflix Simian Army (Chaos / Latency / Chaos Kong / Conformity / Security / Janitor monkeys) as a holistic runtime fitness function. The Checklist Manifesto framing grounds the editorial stance: fitness functions are professional discipline, not surveillance; developers must understand every check before it's imposed.

## 2026-04-16 — Ingest: Fundamentals of Software Architecture Chapter 15 (Space-Based Architecture Style)

**Source**: `raw/fundamentals-of-software-architecture/chapter-15-space-based-architecture-style.md`

Chapter 15 is the sixth Part II style chapter and the book's treatment of **space-based architecture** — the style purpose-built for extreme, variable, unpredictable concurrent load (ticketing drops, auction bursts, booking spikes, > 10,000 concurrent users).
