# IoT Architecture

**Summary**: The data architecture patterns around the Internet of Things — distributed devices that sense their environment, transmit data over constrained networks to gateways and ingestion points, and eventually feed serving patterns that range from scientific analytics to automated control loops. Reis and Housley argue IoT is becoming one of the dominant ways data is generated and consumed.

## Why IoT matters architecturally

Chapter 3 goes deeper on IoT than other patterns because IoT architectures are **less familiar to data engineers** whose careers have been inside business data (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-03-designing-good-data-architecture.md). The smartphone revolution created an IoT swarm overnight; categories since include smart thermostats, car systems, smart speakers, industrial sensors, doorbell cameras, and GPS trackers.

The defining property: **data is generated by devices, not by human keyboard input**, and transmission happens over constrained (low-power, low-bandwidth, sometimes intermittently connected) networks.

## Devices (the "things")

Physical hardware that senses the environment and transmits data. Chapter 3 names the key properties the data engineer should understand (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-03-designing-good-data-architecture.md):

- **What the device does** and **what data it collects**
- Any **edge computation** or **edge ML** it runs before transmission
- **How often** it sends data
- **Failure modes** — device or internet outage, environmental or external factors, and downstream consequences

Devices vary from "send every reading" to "run a model locally and only send interesting events."

## Gateways

An **IoT gateway** is the hub that connects local devices and routes their data securely to internet destinations (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-03-designing-good-data-architecture.md):

- Allows devices to connect using very little power
- Acts as a retention way station
- Manages the internet connection to the final destination

One gateway per physical location typically supports many devices. New low-power WiFi standards are reducing the need for gateways but have not eliminated them.

## Ingestion considerations

IoT ingestion begins at the gateway and flows into an event ingestion architecture. Chapter 3 names the complications that distinguish IoT from business-data ingestion (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-03-designing-good-data-architecture.md):

- **Late-arriving data** — gateways may buffer while offline in remote environments, flushing when WiFi or cellular appears
- **Data structure and schema disparities** across a heterogeneous device fleet
- **Data corruption** from unreliable hardware or networks
- **Connection disruption** as the baseline operational state

These are the source-systems concerns from [[source-systems]] amplified by physical-world constraints.

## Storage

Storage strategy is driven by **latency requirement** (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-03-designing-good-data-architecture.md):

- **Remote scientific sensors** with later analysis → batch object storage is fine
- **Near real-time responses** (home automation, anomaly detection) → message queue or time-series database

The [[data-temperature]] framing from Chapter 2 applies: hot-path IoT data has very different infrastructure needs from archival IoT data.

## Serving

Chapter 3 gives two canonical serving examples (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-03-designing-good-data-architecture.md):

- **Batch scientific application** — data analysed in a cloud warehouse, reported on later
- **Home monitoring** — time-series database for near-real-time detection; stream-processing engine watching for events (fire, outage, break-in); alerts to homeowner or fire department; plus a batch analytics component for monthly reports

### IoT and reverse ETL

One significant IoT serving pattern resembles **[[reverse-etl]]**, though the term is not typically used in IoT context (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-03-designing-good-data-architecture.md). Example: sensors on manufacturing devices send data up; analytical processes find optimisations; new configuration is sent **back down** to the devices to tune their operation.

This closes the loop — data doesn't just flow into analytics, it returns to the physical world as control.

## Relationship to other architecture patterns

IoT architectures compose the other patterns from Chapter 3 rather than replacing them:

- **Ingestion** → [[event-driven-architecture]] with gateways as sources
- **Storage** → mix of time-series stores, [[data-lake]] for archival, [[data-warehousing|warehouse]] for analytics
- **Stream processing** → [[kappa-architecture]] / [[dataflow-model]] shapes for the hot path
- **Serving** → operational analytics back into device fleet (the reverse-ETL shape)
