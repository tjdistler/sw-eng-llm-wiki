# Data Governance

**Summary**: A sub-facet of [[data-management]] — engaging people, processes, and technologies to maximise data value across the organisation while protecting data with appropriate security controls. Chapter 2 structures governance around **three core categories: discoverability, security, and accountability**, with [[data-quality]], [[metadata]], and privacy sitting underneath.

## Definition

The authors quote *Data Governance: The Definitive Guide*: "Data governance is, first and foremost, a data management function to ensure the quality, integrity, security, and usability of the data collected by an organization." They expand: governance engages **people, processes, and technologies** to maximise data value across the organisation while protecting it with appropriate security controls (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-02-the-data-engineering-lifecycle.md).

## What bad governance looks like

Chapter 2's archetypal failure: an analyst gets a report request, doesn't know which tables to use, spends hours digging through dozens of transactional-database tables guessing at useful fields, and produces a "directionally correct" report that neither the analyst nor the report's recipient can trust. The result: the integrity of the analyst — and of the company's data in general — is called into question. Business planning becomes impossible (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-02-the-data-engineering-lifecycle.md).

## Three core categories

Chapter 2 organises governance around three pillars (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-02-the-data-engineering-lifecycle.md):

### 1. Discoverability

End users should have quick and reliable access to data, know where it came from, how it relates to other data, and what it means. The two main subtopics:

- **[[metadata|Metadata]] management.** "Data about data" — the labels, definitions, schemas, owners, and operational records that make data findable and interpretable. Autogenerated or human-generated.
- **[[master-data-management|Master data management (MDM)]].** Consistent entity definitions ("golden records") for business entities.

### 2. Security

Covered in depth as its own undercurrent: see [[data-security]]. Governance intersects by classifying which data is sensitive and who should be able to see what.

### 3. Accountability

Assigning an individual responsibility for a portion of data. The accountable person coordinates governance activities across stakeholders (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-02-the-data-engineering-lifecycle.md):

- The accountable person need not be a data engineer — often a software engineer, product manager, or domain expert.
- They don't have all the resources to maintain quality alone; they coordinate with everyone who touches the data, including data engineers.
- Accountability can happen at any granularity: a table, a log stream, even a single field (e.g., one person owns `customer_id` across many systems).
- The set of all possible values for a given field type is a **data domain** — e.g., the universe of valid customer IDs.

"Managing data quality is tough if no one is accountable for the data in question."

## Privacy, quality, ethics sit under governance

Under the core categories sit subcategories the book treats as their own pages — [[data-quality]], [[metadata]], [[data-ethics]] (privacy).

## When governance works

"When data governance is practiced well, people, processes, and technologies align to treat data as a key business driver; if data issues occur, they are promptly handled." When governance is *accidental* and haphazard, the side effects range from untrusted data to security breaches (source: raw/fundamentals-of-data-engineering/chapter-02-the-data-engineering-lifecycle.md).
